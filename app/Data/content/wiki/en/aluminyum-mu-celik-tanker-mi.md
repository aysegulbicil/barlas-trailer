## Why This Decision Matters

The body material of a tanker directly determines how much you can carry, which products you may carry, what you will spend on maintenance and what the vehicle will be worth second-hand. There are three common options: aluminum, carbon steel and stainless steel. There is no single right answer - the right answer depends on the product being transported and the operating profile.

## Aluminum Body

Aluminum has become the standard material in tanker manufacturing, especially for fuel transport.

- The biggest advantage is weight: an aluminum body is significantly lighter than an equivalent steel one, and the difference goes straight into payload. Since the legal gross weight limit is fixed, every trip carries more product.
- It has natural corrosion resistance against fuel derivatives; the inner surface needs no paint or coating.
- Its thermal conductivity is high; under the sun, product temperature can change faster than in a steel body.
- Weld repair requires expertise: aluminum welding cannot be done at every workshop and calls for a qualified facility.
- The purchase price is higher than carbon steel, but the freight return of the lighter body usually closes that gap over the years.

## Carbon Steel Body

Carbon steel stands out for its mechanical strength and low cost.

- It has the lowest purchase cost of the three options; repairs are widely available and cheap.
- Impact resistance is high, which is an advantage in demanding site conditions (construction sites, mining areas).
- It is prone to corrosion: the inner surface requires a coating or paint suitable for the product carried, and that coating needs periodic inspection.
- The body is heavy, which eats into payload.

## Stainless Steel Body

Stainless steel is indispensable for chemical and food-grade transport.

- It offers the widest cargo compatibility, including acids, bases and aggressive chemicals; it is the de facto standard for many dangerous goods under ADR.
- In food-grade transport (milk, vegetable oil, drinking water) it provides a hygienic inner surface and easy cleaning; it is the most flexible body type for switching between products.
- It is the most expensive option to buy; it weighs more than aluminum and close to carbon steel.
- Because corrosion resistance comes from the material itself rather than a coating, maintenance needs are low and resale value is strong.

## How to Compare Total Cost of Ownership

Looking only at the purchase price is misleading. A sound comparison weighs three items together:

- **Freight return:** the extra payload a lighter body carries per trip x the number of trips per year.
- **Maintenance cost:** coating renewal (carbon steel), specialist welding (aluminum), low upkeep (stainless).
- **Resale value:** stainless and aluminum bodies generally hold their value better.

## Which One Should You Choose?

- **Fuel and light petroleum products:** aluminum, with payload as the priority.
- **Chemicals and dangerous goods:** stainless steel, with cargo compatibility and safety as the priority.
- **Food-grade products:** stainless steel, with hygiene as the priority.
- **Utility loads such as water, irrigation or site fuel:** coated carbon steel, with cost as the priority.
