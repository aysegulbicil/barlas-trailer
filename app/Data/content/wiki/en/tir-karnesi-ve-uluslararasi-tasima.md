## What Is the TIR System?

TIR stands for the French "Transports Internationaux Routiers" (International Road Transports). The TIR system is an international customs transit regime based on the TIR Convention of 1975 and administered under the United Nations Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE). The International Road Transport Union (IRU) and authorised national guaranteeing associations operate the system in practice. A large number of countries are contracting parties, and the system is used across a wide geography spanning Europe, Asia and the Middle East.

The core idea is simple: the cargo is inspected and sealed by customs at the point of departure; at intermediate border crossings it is, as a rule, not reopened or re-examined; the seal is only broken and formalities completed at the customs office of destination.

## What Is the TIR Carnet?

The TIR carnet is the tangible document of this regime in the carrier's hands. Issued for each transport operation, it serves both as a customs declaration and as an international guarantee document.

- Carnets are printed by the IRU and distributed to authorised transport operators through national guaranteeing associations.
- The carnet consists of vouchers (volets) detached at each customs office along the route, so every stage of the journey is recorded.
- The guarantee chain provides international security up to a defined amount against unpaid customs duties and taxes; the guarantee ceiling can differ by country.
- The system is progressively going digital (eTIR); the level of implementation varies from country to country.

## How Does It Simplify Customs?

The facilitation offered by the TIR regime rests on three pillars.

- Approved vehicles: the load compartment must be an approved vehicle or container holding a certificate of approval for secure transport under customs seal, constructed so the cargo cannot be reached without breaking the seal.
- Mutual recognition: the inspection performed and the seal affixed by the customs office of departure are, as a rule, recognised by the other countries en route; no unloading, reloading or full examination is needed at borders. Customs retain the right to inspect in case of suspicion.
- Financial guarantee: thanks to the guarantee chain, transit countries know their duty claims are covered; the carrier does not have to lodge a separate national guarantee in every country.

The result: shorter waiting times at borders, lower processing costs and more predictable transit times.

## Documents Used Alongside the Carnet

The TIR carnet is not sufficient on its own; on an international journey it typically travels together with the following documents.

- The CMR consignment note: the international waybill evidencing the contract of carriage between carrier and shipper.
- Commercial invoice and packing list: show the value and contents of the cargo and underpin customs valuation.
- Export declaration and, where applicable, a certificate of origin (EUR.1, Form A and similar).
- The vehicle's certificate of approval, driver documents and transit permits for the countries on the route.
- Cargo-specific extras: ADR documentation for dangerous goods, veterinary or phytosanitary certificates, ATP paperwork for perishables.

Documentary requirements vary by country and cargo type; verifying current regulations before each journey is essential.
