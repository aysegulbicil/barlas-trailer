## Two Designs, Two Different Missions

The curtainsider and the refrigerated (reefer) trailer are the two most common enclosed-body solutions in road transport, yet they are built for entirely different purposes. The curtainsider is optimised for loading flexibility; the reefer is optimised for protecting cargo temperature. The right choice always follows the nature of the load.

## The Curtainsider

On a curtainsider, the side walls of the body are not fixed panels but tarpaulin curtains sliding on rails. When the curtains are drawn back, the whole side of the deck becomes accessible.

- Loading is possible from the side, the rear and — on curtain-roof versions — from the top; forklifts and cranes work easily.
- Well suited to general cargo: pallets, coils, long profiles, building materials.
- The body is comparatively light, which benefits payload.
- Purchase and maintenance costs are lower than a reefer's; there are few moving parts.
- Its limit is clear: there is no temperature control, and a curtain seals less tightly than a rigid body. Perishable food or pharmaceuticals are out of its scope.

## The Refrigerated Trailer

A reefer combines a closed body of insulated sandwich panels (typically polyurethane-cored) with a cooling unit mounted on the front wall. It is designed for temperature-sensitive cargo: perishable and frozen food, pharmaceuticals, certain chemicals.

- The set temperature is maintained throughout the journey; multi-compartment versions can run different temperature zones.
- In cold-chain transport, international rules (such as the ATP agreement) define equipment classes and periodic testing.
- Loading is normally through the rear doors only; there is no side access, which requires dock-based operations.
- The insulated body and cooling unit add weight, so payload is lower than a curtainsider's.
- Purchase price, fuel or energy consumption and cooling-unit maintenance raise the total cost of ownership.

## The Cost-Flexibility Trade-off

The essence of the comparison: the curtainsider offers high operational flexibility at low cost but gives no temperature guarantee; the reefer delivers that guarantee at the price of extra weight, extra investment and extra running costs.

- If the cargo is insensitive to temperature, running a reefer simply burns money.
- If the cargo is perishable, running a curtainsider puts the load itself at risk — there is no saving in that scenario.
- For finding return loads, the curtainsider addresses a wider freight pool; the reefer, in turn, usually commands higher rates in the cold-chain market. Actual rate levels vary by market and season.

## Which One to Choose?

There is no single winner; the decision follows the cargo. General cargo, palletised industrial goods and mixed part-loads point to the curtainsider; food, pharmaceuticals and anything temperature-sensitive point to the reefer. Carriers that keep both types in their fleet gain the flexibility to serve different freight segments at once.
